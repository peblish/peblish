## Counter-notice

If your material was removed through this process in error, send to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}:

1. Your physical or electronic signature.
2. Identification of the removed material and its previous location.
3. A statement under penalty of perjury that you believe in good faith the removal resulted from mistake or misidentification.
4. Your name, address and telephone number; consent to the jurisdiction of the US Federal District Court for your address, or, if outside the US, any judicial district in which the service provider may be found; and a statement that you will accept service of process from the original notifier or their agent.

We promptly forward a valid counter-notice, including its contact information, to the notifier. We tell them that restoration is planned after 10 business days. Restoration occurs no earlier than 10 and no later than 14 business days after we receive the valid counter-notice, unless we first receive notice that the claimant filed a court action seeking to restrain the alleged infringement. [17 USC 512(g)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512). A separate valid safety or legal restriction may still prevent republication; we explain that ground separately.

Notices may be shared as necessary to process the claim and counter-notice. We minimise unnecessary disclosure, but cannot promise anonymity. Do not send passwords or illegal imagery.

## Repeat infringement

We terminate accounts of repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances and document the decisions. We accommodate and do not interfere with qualifying standard technical measures. [17 USC 512(i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512). We consider substantiated incidents, reversals and abuse of the process; receiving a disputed notice does not automatically make someone an infringer.

This is our own procedure. It does not claim a safe harbour in any country or determine who owns a work. Other copyright complaints can use the same contact channel without claiming to be a US DMCA notice.
